At a glance

The four trade-offs that show up most often when teams evaluate Kentico vs Content.One.

Xperience 13 support ends this year

Kentico Xperience 13 reaches End of Support on December 31, 2026. Until then it gets security hotfixes only, and after that your site keeps running with no fixes at all. Moving to Xperience by Kentico means a new product on new terms, which makes it the natural moment to compare.

Pricing and licensing

Kentico doesn't publish prices, and Xperience by Kentico is licensed per channel, in a Standard or Advanced tier, with AI sold as credit packages. Content.One is quote-based with hosting in the price.

Who actually runs it

Kentico sites are built on ASP.NET Core, Microsoft's web framework, by in-house developers or one of Kentico's 500+ partner agencies, and marketers work from the widgets those developers prepare. On Content.One, marketers build pages visually, with SSO, custom roles and audit logs part of the standard product.

When each wins

Pick Kentico if you want website content, email campaigns, commerce and personalization in one .NET product. Pick Content.One when many locations or chapters need to publish on their own, inside brand guardrails.

Kentico vs Content.One: how they actually differ

Kentico vs Content.One looks like a CMS comparison, but the two products start in different places. Xperience by Kentico is a .NET digital experience platform with content, email, personalization, customer journeys and commerce in one licensed product. Content.One is a federated multisite CMS with hosting included, built for franchise networks, multi-location brands and chapter-based organizations where many sites publish without a developer. So the question is how much of Kentico's suite you'd use, and who on your team would run it.

On Capterra, Kentico holds a 4.3 rating across 48 reviews, with ease of use and customer service both at 4.1. Reviewers like having everything in one place and most often push back on cost and the training advanced tools take, though most of those reviews predate 2024 and describe the older Kentico CMS rather than Xperience by Kentico.

Kentico pricing comes from a quote built on channels, a Standard or Advanced tier and AIRA credits, and a second full website needs Advanced. The date matters more, because Kentico Xperience 13 reaches End of Support on December 31, 2026, with security hotfixes only until then and nothing from January 1, 2027. Its successor, Xperience by Kentico, is a separate product, and while the Kentico Migration Tool moves content, Kentico's upgrade docs say code and customizations move by hand because the page API and data structure are completely different. That rebuild runs on ASP.NET Core, which 19.7% of respondents used in the Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey, against 44.7% for React. (Our Kentico alternative post covers why teams leave.)

Content.One starts from the other end. Hosting and global CDN come with it, and weekly updates reach every customer with zero downtime, so there's no upgrade project to plan. It's built for federated multisite, where headquarters sets templates and permissions and each location or chapter publishes its own pages, and the Salvation Army used it to go from five CMSs to one across 3,000+ locations, with traffic up 50%. Our MCP server, generally available since June 2026, lets marketers build pages by prompting Claude or Gemini, with every action checked against editor permissions. KentiCopilot's MCP servers are aimed at developers, and Kentico's docs keep the management API behind them to local development instances.

Kentico comes out ahead on breadth. Xperience by Kentico has native email marketing with an Email Builder and autoresponders, built-in commerce with carts, checkout and promotions, personalization tied to contact profiles, and the AIRA Agentic Marketing Suite. Kentico has also been making moves to help its users, with single-tenant SaaS, weekly hotfixes and monthly refreshes instead of major versions, ISO 27001 certification and a SOC 2 Type II audit, and a KentiCopilot plugin that guides the Xperience 13 migration. With more than 500 agency partners and published support response times, it's an established choice for Microsoft shops.

So it comes down to who does the work. If you have .NET developers or a trusted Kentico partner and want email, commerce and personalization in the same product as your website, Xperience by Kentico is a sensible next step. If you run dozens or hundreds of location or chapter sites and want marketers publishing without a .NET build behind each change, the Xperience 13 deadline is a good moment to move to Content.One.

When Kentico is the better pick

Kentico is the stronger choice if you want one .NET product for website content, email campaigns, commerce and personalization, you have in-house ASP.NET Core developers or a long-standing Kentico partner, your security team needs private cloud or on-premises hosting, or you're already partway through the move from Kentico Xperience 13 to Xperience by Kentico.

Frequently asked questions

What teams ask most when they weigh Kentico against Content.One.

Is Content.One a good Kentico alternative?

Yes, for franchise, multi-location and multi-chapter teams, and the timing works in your favor. Moving to Xperience by Kentico also means rebuilding your code and customizations, so both paths start with a project, and the choice is which platform that project lands on.

Kentico vs Content.One: which costs more?

Neither vendor publishes prices, so compare quotes line by line. Kentico doesn't charge per admin user or visitor, so ask how many channels your sites need, which tier and cloud level fit and how many AIRA credits you'll use, then add the partner's build fee. For Content.One, ask what the quote covers for hosting, onboarding and any on-demand developer time.

Do I need .NET developers?

For Kentico, yes, since Xperience by Kentico installs as NuGet packages inside an ASP.NET Core application that your team or partner maintains. Content.One doesn't need a .NET team, and when you do want developer time, you can engage our on-demand developers directly instead of signing up an agency.

What happens when Kentico Xperience 13 support ends?

Your site keeps running after December 31, 2026, but from January 1, 2027 Kentico ships no more fixes for it, security fixes included, and stops taking support tickets for the version. Its lifecycle policy also ends Kentico's liability for Xperience 13 on that date, so any incident after it is yours to handle.

Does Content.One do personalization like Kentico?

Content.One picks the right page variant on the server by audience rules like location, device, referrer or UTM tag, so visitors don't see a flicker and search engines still get a fast page. Kentico's personalization reaches further into email and contact profiles, because both live in the same product.

Why are Content.One reviews listed under Zesty.io?

Content.One was called Zesty.io until November 2025, so most of its G2 and Capterra reviews were written under the old name, and the Capterra profile address still carries it. It's the same product under a new name.

Is migrating from Kentico hard?

Most of the effort sits in templates, widgets and custom .NET code, since content can be exported and mapped while code has to be rebuilt for the new platform. The estimator at migration.content.one sizes a move off Kentico in a few minutes.

Which is better for multi-site or multi-brand teams?

Kentico runs each extra full website as a main channel on the Advanced tier, and small microsites or sub-brand sites can go on Microchannels, which Kentico caps by content volume. On Content.One, headquarters shares templates and approval workflows with every location, and activity logs show who changed what.
