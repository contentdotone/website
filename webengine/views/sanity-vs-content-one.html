Sanity vs Content.One: how they actually differ

Let's be fair to Sanity first, because it earns its reputation. It's been rated the #1 headless CMS on G2 for four years running, sits around 4.7 across 900+ reviews, and the developer experience is about as good as the category gets. Schema is defined in TypeScript, GROQ handles complex queries in a single request, real-time collaboration is built in, and Sanity Studio is an open-source React app you can shape into whatever your editors need. For a product team with engineers, that's a strong hand.

The thing to understand is who does the work. Sanity is developer-first by design, and the same flexibility that product teams love is the part a marketing team feels as friction. You define the model in code, you query it with GROQ, and you still build and host your own frontend. Sanity is the content backend and the Content Lake. The frontend, the hosting, and the devops to keep it running are on you. That's normal for headless. It's also a cost and a dependency that never shows up on the pricing page.

Then there's how spend grows. Sanity CMS pricing starts free, moves to Growth at $15 per seat a month, and meters usage on top, API requests, documents, and bandwidth, with overages once you cross the included amounts (Sanity's pricing page has the current numbers). The part that catches enterprise buyers is governance. SSO and SAML, custom roles, and audit logs live on the Enterprise tier. If you want SAML without going full Enterprise, the Growth add-on runs roughly $1,399 a month on its own (per an April 2026 pricing breakdown). For a single marketing site, none of that bites. For a brand running dozens of sites with real access-control needs, it adds up fast.

This is where Content.One is built differently, and it's worth being specific. Content.One includes hosting and global CDN delivery, so there's no separate host to manage or patch. It's designed around federated multisite, so one team runs many sites and brands from a single platform instead of wiring datasets and projects together. It leans on visual editing, generative AI, and a native MCP server, so a marketer can publish a page without filing a developer ticket. And SSO, custom roles, and audit logs sit in the standard product, not behind an Enterprise contract.

Reviews reflect the split. Content.One's reviews currently sit under its former name, Zesty.io, on G2 and Capterra, since the rebrand is still rolling out. On Capterra the recurring themes are ease of use for non-developers and hands-on support, with one customer saying the team "goes above and beyond." On the Sanity side, the praise is consistent on developer experience and collaboration, and the most common caveat is just as consistent: you need a developer to get the most out of it, and GROQ has a learning curve.

None of this makes Sanity the wrong call. If your team is engineering-led and you want the most flexible content model in the category, Sanity is a genuinely excellent choice, and its AI direction with Content Agent and the MCP server is ahead of most of the field. The honest decision is about who's publishing. If it's developers shipping through APIs, Sanity fits. If it's marketers running a lot of sites without waiting on engineering, the included hosting, federated multisite, and no-ticket publishing in Content.One tend to matter more. When Sanity is the better pick

Sanity is the stronger choice if your team is developer-led and comfortable in code, you want the most flexible content model in the category with schema defined in TypeScript, real-time collaboration and a fully custom editing UI are core to how you work, or you already have your frontend and hosting handled and just want the best structured-content backend you can get.

Frequently asked questions

The questions that come up most often when teams compare Sanity and Content.One.

Is Content.One a good Sanity alternative?

For teams running multiple sites or publishing without heavy developer involvement, yes. Sanity is the stronger pick if you're developer-led and want maximum control of the content model in code.

How much does Sanity cost compared to Content.One?

Sanity CMS pricing is Free, then Growth at $15 per seat a month, with usage-based charges on API requests, documents, and bandwidth, and Enterprise quoted custom. Content.One is quote-based with no public tiers. Factor in that Content.One includes hosting, which you pay for separately with Sanity.

Does Sanity include hosting?

No. Sanity hosts your content in the Content Lake and hosts Sanity Studio, but you build and host the actual website frontend yourself. Content.One includes hosting and CDN delivery.

Do I need a developer to use Sanity?

To set it up, yes. Schemas and the Studio are configured in code, and querying uses GROQ. Once a developer has built it, editors can publish without code. Content.One is built so a non-developer can model and publish from the start.

Does Sanity work with AI agents?

Yes. Sanity has a native MCP server at mcp.sanity.io plus Agent Context, so AI agents can query and update content. Content.One also ships a native MCP server, so both platforms support agent-driven content operations.

Does Content.One support SSO and custom roles?

Yes, both are in the standard product, with permissions you can set per content type. On Sanity, SSO/SAML and custom roles require the Enterprise tier or a paid Growth add-on.

Why are Content.One reviews listed under Zesty.io?

Content.One was rebranded from Zesty.io, and the review profiles on G2 and Capterra haven't fully migrated yet.

Which is better for franchise or multi-location brands?

Content.One is built around federated multisite, so one team manages many sites from a single platform. Sanity handles multiple sites through separate datasets and projects, which adds setup and management overhead at scale.
