At a glance

The four things that come up most when a multi-site network compares Webflow and Content.One.

Every published site needs its own plan

Webflow charges per published site. The Premium plan is $25 a month billed yearly, or $39 monthly. An 80-location network is 80 Premium plans, roughly $24,000 a year in site plans alone, before Workspace seats and bandwidth.

Pricing sits in two layers, plus add-ons

Site plans cover what a published site can do. Workspace plans cover who can work on it, priced per seat. Optimize, Analyze, and Localize bill separately again, so the plan price is rarely the invoice.

Content is scoped to the site it lives in

CMS Collections belong to one site. There is no shared content model across a network, so a corporate update to 80 locations is 80 edits or an API script.

Both platforms are serious about MCP

Webflow shipped MCP 2.0 in July 2026, free to all users, with role-based permissions and full activity logging on agent actions. Content.One ships a native MCP server with per-request permissions. Nobody is behind here.

Webflow vs Content.One: one site done beautifully, or a network done once

Webflow earns its reputation. The Designer gives a marketing team genuine control over layout, typography, and interactions without a developer in the loop, the hosting is fast and included, and the developer surface has grown well past what a website builder normally offers, with the Data API, code components, and Webflow Cloud all sitting under the same roof. The MCP server deserves specific credit too. MCP 2.0 landed in July 2026 with role inheritance, per-site Agent Instructions, and logging on every agent action, and Webflow made it free to everyone rather than gating it behind a tier.

The constraint is the unit. Webflow's model is one site, priced and governed as one site. A Site plan covers a published site, a Workspace plan covers the people working on it, and add-ons like Localize and Optimize attach to individual sites again. That structure is coherent for a company with one website, or an agency running client sites that have nothing to do with each other.

A network is a different problem. When corporate owns 80 locations, the platform question is not how good the design tool is, it is what happens when legal changes a disclosure and every location needs it live by Friday. On Webflow that is 80 edits, a script against the Data API, or an Enterprise conversation. Content.One is built around structured content shared across properties, so the same change is one publish action against the properties in scope. For the deeper pattern, see our federated multisite solution and the write-ups on managing 100+ websites from one platform and why content operations break in multi-location businesses.

Where Content.One takes the lead

Start with the cost curve, because it is the part buyers underestimate. Webflow's per-site pricing is a bargain for one site and linear for a network. Eighty locations on Premium is roughly $24,000 a year in site plans before anyone has a seat, and Workspace seats, bandwidth add-ons above 50GB per site, and per-locale Localize charges all stack on top of that. Webflow's own pricing documentation shows a single 500GB site landing at $199 a month once bandwidth add-ons are applied, which is a useful sense of how the add-on layer behaves at volume.

Governance is the second gap, and it is architectural rather than commercial. There is no shared content layer across Webflow sites, so brand consistency across a network depends on people doing the same thing 80 times correctly. Even the Team plan, which bundles a Workspace and a site together, includes a single site, with additional sites handled as a sales conversation. Content.One treats the network as the unit, with corporate publishing shared content once and each location controlling what is local to it.

Local editing is the third. Webflow deprecated its legacy Editor, so content changes now happen inside the Designer, which is a design tool with a real learning curve. Handing that to a franchisee who needs to update opening hours is how brand drift starts. Content.One gives location and chapter editors guided authoring scoped to what they are allowed to change.

When Webflow wins

If design control is the deciding factor, pick Webflow. Nothing in this comparison changes that, and a marketing team that wants pixel-level control over a flagship site with custom interactions will be happier there. Content.One is not trying to win that argument.

Webflow is also the better call for a single site or a small handful of unrelated ones, where per-site pricing works in your favour and the federated model is solving a problem you do not have. Teams doing serious frontend or app work should look hard at Webflow Cloud and code components, and anyone running an agent-heavy workflow should know that Webflow's MCP implementation is among the most complete on the market, with governance features some enterprise platforms have not shipped yet.

Frequently asked questions

The questions that come up most when multi-location teams compare Webflow and Content.One.

How much does Webflow cost for 50 or 100 sites?

Each published site needs its own Site plan. At the Premium rate of $25 a month billed yearly, 50 sites is about $15,000 a year and 100 sites is about $30,000 a year in site plans alone, before Workspace seats, bandwidth add-ons, or per-locale localization.

Does Webflow support multisite?

Not as a self-serve product. Sites are independent, each with its own plan, CMS Collections, and settings. Multi-site arrangements are handled through the Team plan or Enterprise, both of which are quote-only.

Can I update content across every Webflow site at once?

Not from the CMS. Collections belong to a single site, so a network-wide change means editing each site or writing against the Data API. This is the main structural difference from federated multisite.

Does Webflow work with AI agents and MCP?

Yes, and it does it well. Webflow's MCP 2.0 launched in July 2026, is free on all plans, and applies existing roles and permissions to agent actions with full activity logging. Content.One also ships a native MCP server, so both platforms support agent-driven content work.

What changed in Webflow's May 2026 pricing?

Webflow merged the CMS and Business Site plans into a single Premium plan at $25 a month billed yearly, raised CMS items to 20,000 and static pages to 300, and reduced base bandwidth on former Business sites from 100GB to 50GB. It also added AI credits to every Workspace plan.

Why are Content.One reviews listed under Zesty.io?

Content.One was rebranded from Zesty.io, and the review profiles on G2 and Capterra have not fully migrated yet.

What does moving from Webflow to Content.One involve?

Content.One runs the migration alongside your live sites, and Engineer on Demand covers the implementation work directly rather than routing it through a certified partner. You can get a scoped estimate at migration.content.one.
