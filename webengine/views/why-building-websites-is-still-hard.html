Why knowing everything still makes building websites hard

RA

Randy Apuzzo

Founder & CEO, Content.One · 25 years building websites

You'd think with all the knowledge in the world at our fingertips, building a website would be easy. It's not.

I've been building websites and working with content management systems for more than 25 years. The difference between me and someone starting out today isn't access to information — it's the scar tissue. It's knowing what actually matters when things break at 2 a.m.

Go back to the 90s. We were hand-coding static HTML in Notepad. Then came Dreamweaver, CSS frameworks, WordPress themes, and the explosion of no-code tools. Each wave promised to make it simpler.

Today, AI tools like Framer AI, Lovable, v0 by Vercel, Wix AI, and OpenAI's Sites can generate a full website from a single prompt. The speed is incredible.

Yet people still struggle. AI often produces generic designs that all look the same, hallucinates broken code, ignores SEO and security best practices, and ships sites that are painful to update or scale.

AI gives you the facts. It doesn't give you the judgment that only comes from shipping hundreds of sites. That gap — between knowledge and skill — is exactly why experienced developers and platforms like ours are still in demand.

The tools got easier. The craft didn't.

Navigating the future

So how do you move forward? Use AI to accelerate the early stages — generating ideas, layouts, first drafts. Then partner with experienced professionals who understand strategy, user experience, performance, and long-term maintenance.

The winners won't be the teams who use AI alone or humans alone. They'll be the teams that combine both intelligently.

If you want a website that actually grows your business — instead of becoming another AI-generated template in a sea of sameness — work with people who've been doing this for decades. The tech changes. Real expertise doesn't.
