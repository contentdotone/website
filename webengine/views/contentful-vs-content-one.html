Contentful vs Content.One: how they actually differ

Most teams don't leave Contentful because it's a bad headless CMS. They leave because the bill and the limits start shaping how they work. On G2, where Contentful holds a 4.2 rating across 321 reviews, the praise is consistent: clean UI, strong APIs, flexible content modeling. The complaints are just as consistent. One reviewer put the pricing plainly, saying it "might seem a bit expensive, especially for small teams." That tracks with how the plans are built. There's a free tier, then a jump to Lite at $300 a month, with limits on API calls, users, locales, and content types. Hit a ceiling and the fix is usually a tier upgrade, not a small add-on.

The content-type cap is the one that surprises people. The free Starter Space stops at 25 content types, and Reddit threads from Contentful users describe hitting the limit with nowhere to go but a pricier plan. As one put it, "you can quickly hit that limit." For a single marketing site that's fine. For a brand running dozens of sites or a complex model, it becomes a planning problem you carry for years. (For a longer write-up of where teams typically land after Contentful, see our Contentful alternatives post.)

Then there's hosting. Contentful is the content backend. You still bring your own frontend, your own hosting, and the devops to keep it running. That's normal for headless, but it's a cost and a dependency that doesn't show up on the pricing page.

This is where Content.One is built differently, and it's worth being specific. Content.One includes hosting and global CDN delivery, so there's no separate host to manage or patch. It's designed around federated multisite, so one team can run many sites and brands from a single platform instead of juggling separate Spaces. And it leans on visual editing, generative AI, and a native MCP server, so a marketer can publish a page without filing a developer ticket. SSO, custom roles, and audit logs sit in the standard product rather than behind a Premium contract.

Reviewers reflect that difference. Content.One's reviews currently sit under its former name, Zesty.io, on G2 and Capterra, since the rebrand is still rolling out. On Capterra, the recurring themes are ease of use for non-developers and hands-on support, with one customer saying the team "goes above and beyond." Several note they stopped running their own software and database updates after moving.

None of this makes Contentful the wrong call for everyone. If you've got a developer-led team that wants mature, well-documented APIs, a large app marketplace, and a proven record at enterprise scale, Contentful earns its reputation. Its GraphQL support and integration breadth are genuinely strong.

The honest split is about who's doing the work. If your day-to-day is developers shipping through APIs, Contentful fits. If it's marketers publishing across a lot of sites without waiting on engineering, the included hosting, federated multisite, and no-ticket publishing in Content.One tend to matter more than one more API feature. That's the real decision, not a feature checklist.

When Contentful is the better pick

Contentful is the stronger choice if your team is developer-led and API-first, you want the largest app and integration marketplace in the category, your content model is stable and unlikely to outgrow the type limits, or you already have your frontend and hosting handled and just need a proven content backend.

Frequently asked questions

The questions that come up most often when teams compare Contentful and Content.One.

Is Content.One a good Contentful alternative?

For teams running multiple sites or publishing without heavy developer involvement, yes. Contentful still wins on integration breadth and marketplace depth.

How much does Content.One cost compared to Contentful?

Contentful publishes a free tier, Lite at $300 a month, and custom Premium pricing. Content.One is quote-based with no public tiers. Factor in that Content.One includes hosting, which is a separate cost with Contentful.

Does Contentful include hosting?

No. Contentful is the content backend, so you provide the frontend and hosting. Content.One includes hosting and CDN delivery.

Why are Content.One reviews listed under Zesty.io?

Content.One was rebranded from Zesty.io, and the review profiles on G2 and Capterra haven't fully migrated yet.

Does Content.One support SSO and custom roles?

Yes, both are in the standard product, with permissions you can set per content type. On Contentful, SSO and custom roles require a Premium or custom plan.

Is migrating from Contentful to Content.One hard?

Effort depends on how many content types and locales you're moving. You can get an estimate from the migration tool at migration.content.one.

Which is better for franchise or multi-location brands?

Content.One is built around federated multisite, so one team manages many sites from a single platform. Contentful handles this through multiple Spaces, which adds management overhead at scale.
