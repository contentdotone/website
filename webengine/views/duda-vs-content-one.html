At a glance

The four things that come up most when a multi-site network compares Duda and Content.One.

Duda prices per site, and it adds up

Plans run $25 to $199 a month on monthly billing, with one site included on Basic and Team, and four on Agency and White Label. Every site after that is $17 a month. An 80-site network lands around $1,361 a month on monthly billing, or roughly $13,400 a year on annual.

Built for agencies, by design

Duda's own pricing FAQ describes the platform as built for businesses offering websites to other businesses. The account model follows from that, with team seats capped at six outside the Custom plan and client accounts scoped to the specific sites they are assigned.

Sites are separate, not federated

Duda gives you reusable design assets like custom templates and sections. Content itself lives inside each site, so a policy change across 80 locations means 80 edits, an API script, or a Custom plan conversation.

Both ship MCP, on different terms

Duda's MCP access starts on the Agency plan and runs on a metered pool of 150 monthly AI credits shared across all paid tiers. Content.One's MCP server is part of the platform, with permissions applying per request.

Duda vs Content.One: one platform sells websites, the other governs a network

Duda is good at what it was built for. Agencies and SaaS platforms use it to produce a high volume of well-built, fast, SEO-sound sites for small business clients, and the reviews back that up, with ease of use and ease of setup among its strongest scores on G2. Hosting is included, the editor is genuinely pleasant, and the white-label layer lets an agency put the whole platform under its own brand.

The model underneath all of that is a reseller model. Duda's pricing FAQ says the platform is built for businesses offering websites to other businesses, and the account structure reflects it. You buy platform access, then buy sites individually. Team members see every site in the account. Clients see only the sites assigned to them and cannot touch anything at platform level. That split works when the sites belong to different companies who happen to share a builder.

A franchise network is not that. Neither is a 60-storefront retailer or an 80-chapter nonprofit. Those organizations own every property, and the thing they need most is for corporate to change something once and have it appear everywhere it should, while each location still controls what is local to it. Content.One is built around that shape, with structured content shared across properties and role-based publishing that lets a franchisee update hours without being able to edit the brand. For a deeper look at how the pattern actually works, see our federated multisite solution and the write-ups on managing 100+ websites from one platform and why content operations break in multi-location businesses.

Where Content.One takes the lead

The clearest gap is the shared content model. Duda's reusable pieces are design assets, so templates and sections carry look and feel between sites while the content inside each site stays local to it. Update a compliance disclosure across 80 locations on Duda and you are editing 80 sites, writing against the partner API, or negotiating a Custom plan. On Content.One the same change is one publish action against the properties in scope, which is the difference between an afternoon and a fortnight.

Governance is the second gap, and it shows up as a hard number. Duda caps team seats at six outside the Custom plan, and client accounts by design cannot reach platform-level features. A corporate marketing team of eight running a network of chapter editors does not fit that shape without moving to a negotiated tier, at which point the published pricing that made Duda attractive stops applying.

Migration is the third. Duda documents its own path for large portfolios honestly: rebuild one or two sites yourself with Import Content, hand a dozen or two to the Experts program, and talk to sales above a hundred. For a network moving off WordPress multisite or an aging DXP, that means a rebuild project with an implementation partner attached. Content.One runs migrations through Engineer on Demand, which is the same engineers who run the platform rather than a separate systems integrator engagement.

When Duda wins

If you are an agency, a SaaS platform, or a host selling websites to small business clients, Duda is the better fit and it is not close. The white-label layer, the client billing, the per-site economics, and the Simple Editor all exist for that business model, and Content.One does not compete for it.

Duda also wins on transparency and on speed to first site. The pricing is published, the trial takes minutes, and a small network of ten or fifteen sites with mostly independent content will run on Duda for a few hundred dollars a month with very little to argue about. If your locations genuinely operate as separate businesses with separate content, the federated model is solving a problem you do not have yet. Duda's SEO work is also legitimately good, from Core Web Vitals through to llms.txt support, so nobody should switch away from it expecting a search improvement on its own.

Frequently asked questions

The questions that come up most when multi-location teams compare Duda and Content.One.

How much does Duda cost for a network of 50 or 100 sites?

Take the plan price, then add $17 a month for every site past the ones included. On the Agency plan at $69 a month with four sites included, 50 sites is about $851 a month and 100 sites is about $1,701 a month on monthly billing. Annual billing brings both down by roughly a quarter.

Is Duda a CMS?

It manages website content, so in the broad sense yes, and G2 lists it under web content management. In practice it works as a site-scoped builder rather than a structured content platform, so content lives inside each site instead of in a shared model several sites can draw from.

Can Duda publish a change across all my locations at once?

Not through the editor. Templates and sections carry design between sites, but content changes happen per site unless you script them through the partner API or arrange it on a Custom plan.

Does Duda work with AI agents and MCP?

Yes. Duda ships an MCP server, with access starting on the Agency plan, and its AI features draw on a pool of 150 monthly credits with paid top-ups. Content.One also ships a native MCP server, included in the platform, so both support agent-driven content work.

Why are Content.One reviews listed under Zesty.io?

Content.One was rebranded from Zesty.io, and the review profiles on G2 and Capterra have not fully migrated yet.

What does moving from Duda to Content.One involve?

Content.One runs the migration in parallel with your live sites, and Engineer on Demand covers the implementation work directly. You can get a scoped estimate at migration.content.one.

Should a franchise network use Duda or Content.One?

If franchisees buy and run their own sites independently, Duda handles that well. If corporate owns the brand standards and needs to push updates across every location while letting each one manage what is local, that is what federated multisite is for.
